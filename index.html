How SELCAL Works

The Code

Every tail is assigned a unique 4-letter SELCAL code, built from 16 possible letters — A through S, skipping I, N, and O (they're too easy to confuse with 1, 4, and 0). That gives 65,780 possible 4-letter combinations, more than enough for every airframe in the fleet.

The Tones

Each letter maps to one specific audio tone. The 16 tones aren't spaced randomly — they climb geometrically from 312.6 Hz up to 1479.1 Hz, each one about 10.9% higher than the last:

fn = fA × r(n−1)    r ≈ 1.109

That even spacing keeps every tone easy to tell apart from its neighbors, while keeping the whole set inside the same voiceband your HF/VHF radio already carries.

The Transmission

SELCAL never sends all 4 letters at once. It sends two dual-tone pulses back to back — each pulse is two frequencies playing together, which is just their waveforms added:

s(t) = sin(2πf1t) + sin(2πf2t)

Pulse 1 carries letters 1–2 for about 1.0 second, then a ~0.2 second gap, then pulse 2 carries letters 3–4 for another 1.0 second. That's exactly what happens when you hit "Test SELCAL Tone" above.

The Decode

Your aircraft's SELCAL decoder is always listening, filtering the incoming audio through narrow bandpass filters tuned to those 16 tones. It only chimes when it sees its own two exact tone-pairs, in the right order, held for the right duration — everyone else's calls pass through silently.
